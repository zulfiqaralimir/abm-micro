Asad Zaman announced that the ABM Micro course begins September 23, 2026 at
Ibn Haldun University in Istanbul, running through December.

### Why a live, unfinished course is worth watching

Zaman is teaching this
material in this form for the first time, and says that's a feature, not a
caveat: for anyone deciding whether to teach a similar course themselves,
<mark className="rounded bg-yellow-200 px-1 text-ink">seeing what actually
breaks in the classroom is more useful than a polished, already-debugged
version would be</mark>. If the course were already perfect, you'd
only see what works. Since it's brand new, you get to see
<mark className="rounded bg-yellow-200 px-1 text-ink">what goes wrong too —
and how it gets fixed</mark>. **For someone thinking about teaching it
themselves, that's more helpful than only seeing the finished, clean
version.**

### Why microeconomics

<mark className="rounded bg-yellow-200 px-1 text-ink">Plenty
of courses could draw on this approach, but Zaman singles out microeconomics
as unusually well suited</mark> — enough so that
<strong className="text-blue-600">the entire standard micro
curriculum could be rebuilt with agent-based modeling as its core method,
rather than added on as a supplement.</strong>

### Supplementary material, in three tracks

Zaman plans three kinds of
follow-up material, and **which comes first depends on what people ask
for:**

- **For teachers** — practical guidance on running the course, based on what
  actually happens this term
- **For researchers and thesis students** — the many open extensions, since
  <strong className="text-blue-600">very little of this ground has been
  worked yet</strong>
- **For anyone interested in the underlying ideas** — the full methodological
  and philosophical argument that the course puts into practice but doesn't
  spell out directly

He's specifically
<strong className="text-blue-600">inviting responses from anyone planning to
teach something similar, on which of these would be most useful to
them.</strong>
